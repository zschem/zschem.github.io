Research

Our research program centers on the rational design, controlled RAFT synthesis, and supramolecular assembly of lipid-based and polymeric biomaterials engineered to interface with biological membranes. By exploiting alternating and block vinyl ether–maleic anhydride (VEMA) copolymers, we formulate monodisperse polymer–lipid nanodiscs (VEMALPs and SMALPs) with programmable diameters from 60 nm down to 3 nm.

Using nucleophilic ring-opening bioconjugation with PEG chains, amino acids, and charge-tunable modifiers (VEMADs), as well as ultra-mild (<60 s) special class of aliphatic VEMA formulations, we preserve fragile membrane proteins (human TRPV1, KCNE1, KCNE4, gp28) across pH 3.0–9.0 and up to 30 mM Mg2+ while developing antiviral bilayer-disrupting networks and liposomal nanotraps.

Team & Collaborations

Our research is highly interdisciplinary, bridging synthetic polymer chemistry, bioconjugation, and membrane biophysics in the laboratories of Prof. Dominik Konkolewicz, Prof. Rick Page, and Prof. Gary Lorigan at Miami University.

Synthetic chemists apply reversible addition-fragmentation chain transfer (RAFT) polymerization and post-polymerization functionalization to create tailored macromolecular scaffolds, working side-by-side with biophysicists utilizing CW-EPR, DOSY NMR, SEC/GPC, DLS, and TEM. Over the past five years, Muhammad Zeeshan Shah has directly trained and mentored 11 undergraduate and 2 graduate researchers, yielding 5+ co-authored manuscripts, and served as President of the ACS POLY/PMSE Cincinnati Chapter (2024–2025).
